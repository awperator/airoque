Service 09

Work Instructions — SWIs & VWIs

Instructions are only controls if someone uses them. AiroQue writes standard and visual work instructions for the workstation — at the reading level, photo density, format and language your floor actually needs.

1.0 Standard

The test is whether it gets opened

Work instructions fail in a recognizable way. They are written by someone who already knows the job, for a reader who does not, and they skip the parts that are obvious to the author — which are exactly the parts that go wrong. Then they are printed, put in a binder, and the job is learned by watching someone else.

An instruction that is used has a few properties: it is in the sequence the work is performed, it names the tooling and settings rather than implying them, it says what “good” looks like at each checkpoint, and it fits at the station without being folded twice.

What goes into each instruction

Setup

Tooling, fixturing, program or recipe identification, machine settings, material and consumables, and anything that must be verified before the first part.

Sequence

Steps in performance order, each doing one thing, with the process parameters that matter stated at the step where they apply rather than collected in a preamble.

Checkpoints and acceptance

Where the operator verifies, what they check, against what criterion, and what gets recorded — cross-referenced to the Control Plan so the two documents cannot drift apart.

Reaction

What to do when the check fails, at the level of detail the operator can act on without leaving the station to find out.

Control

Revision identification, approval and effectivity, in the format your document-control process expects.

Visual instructions

Where the work is visual — orientation, seating, routing, sequence, appearance of a good joint or coating — photographs with annotation carry more than a paragraph does. AiroQue can work from photographs you supply, or specify the shots needed so someone on site can take them.

3.0 Tailoring

Written for the people at the station

This is the deliverable where tailoring matters most, because the audience is the least forgiving and has the least time.

Your floor, your format

AiroQue asks how instructions are consumed before writing any: printed and laminated, on a tablet, on a screen at the cell, or pulled from the ERP with the traveller. Format follows that answer.

Reading level and language

Written plainly, with sentence length and vocabulary matched to your workforce. Bilingual side-by-side or separate-language issues where that is what the floor needs.

Photo density

Text-led with a few reference images, or photo-led with a picture at nearly every step. Denser visuals cost more to produce and are worth it for high-turnover or highly visual work.

Physical format

Single-page station sheet, booklet, laminated card, tablet-friendly PDF, or content structured for your ERP or MES so operators see it where they already look.

Terminology

Your names for tools, fixtures, machines, stations and departments — including the informal names people actually use, where those are unambiguous.

Callouts and warnings

FOD, ESD, torque, handling, safety and cleanliness callouts placed where your plant conventionally puts them, at the step they apply to.

Document control

Header blocks, numbering, revision identification, approval fields and effectivity formatted to match the rest of your controlled documentation exactly.
