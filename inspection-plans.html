Service 06

Inspection Plans

An inspection plan turns a drawing into instructions someone can execute the same way twice. AiroQue develops the plan, the method selection behind it, and the format your inspection group and customer need to see.

1.0 Function

The bridge between engineering requirements and inspection activity

A drawing states what the product must be. It does not state how anyone confirms it, with what, how often, or against what acceptance limit once measurement uncertainty is taken into account. That gap is where inconsistent inspection comes from: two inspectors, two methods, two results, one argument.

The inspection plan closes it by making the method a decision that was made once, deliberately, by someone who looked at the tolerance, the feature, the datum scheme and the equipment actually available.

How characteristics become a plan

Extraction

Every requirement on the drawing, in referenced specifications and in the contract is identified — dimensional, geometric, material, process, finish, marking, packaging. Notes are requirements too, and they are the ones most often missed.

Method selection

Each characteristic is assigned a method that can actually resolve it. Tolerance against gauge discrimination, feature accessibility, fixturing, datum establishment, part deflection, surface condition and throughput all constrain what is realistic.

Sampling and frequency

Set against risk, criticality, process stability, run length and customer requirements — and stated in a way that survives contact with a production schedule.

Acceptance and reaction

What constitutes conformance, and what happens when a result falls outside it, including who is notified and what is done with the parts already produced.

AiroQue develops inspection planning documentation. AiroQue does not provide First Article Inspection services.

2.0 Content

What the plan defines

Per characteristic

  • Characteristic identification and reference
  • Drawing requirement and location
  • Specification requirement where referenced
  • Inspection method
  • Measurement equipment and fixturing
  • Sampling frequency and sample size
  • Acceptance criteria
  • Special-characteristic designation where applicable

Across the process

  • In-process inspection points and their purpose
  • Final inspection content
  • Documentation and record requirements
  • Reaction requirements on nonconformance
  • Equipment calibration dependencies
  • Handling of characteristics verified by outside processors
  • Characteristics verified by process control rather than measurement

3.0 Tailoring

Numbering, structure and wording set by your inspection group

Inspection documentation is used under time pressure by people who did not write it. It has to match the conventions they already know.

Written for the people executing it

AiroQue asks how your inspectors work before deciding how the plan is laid out — what they read first, what they record, and where the plan sits relative to the traveller and the drawing.

Characteristic numbering

Follows your existing balloon or characteristic numbering convention, your customer's required scheme, or an operation-based scheme — so references match across the drawing, plan and records.

Structure

One consolidated plan, separate in-process and final plans, plans issued per operation, or a plan keyed to a marked-up drawing, depending on how your inspection is organized.

Method detail level

Method named only, or with setup, datum establishment and fixturing described — heavier where the measurement is difficult or has caused disputes before.

Equipment references

Generic equipment types, or specific gauge identification numbers tied to your calibration system, if you want the plan to enforce which gauge is used.

Output format

Excel, PDF, a form matching your inspection records, or structured content for entry into your ERP or inspection software.

Terminology

Your names for departments, stations, gauges and record forms, so nothing has to be mentally translated at the bench.
